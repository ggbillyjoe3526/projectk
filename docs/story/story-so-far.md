## 1. The premise

**Settled.** A man comes back by ferry to the remote Orkney/Shetland-style island he was taken from at five, for the funeral of a father he barely remembers. He sits the night by the coffin without knowing the old rituals. At the funeral his father's body is gone and the islanders turn on him. A storm cancels the ferry, and after the first great fight the pier becomes too dangerous to use, so the ferry company reroutes it. He survives from his father's house on a tidal islet and fights his way across the island to the only other harbour the ferry can use. There he either leaves, or stays to take up his father's watch.

**Proposed: names.** The island is **Haugsay** (a placeholder, Old Norse for "mound island"). The father's tidal islet is **the Brough**.

## 2. How the story is told

**Settled**
- **No cutscenes, ever.** Everything comes from talking to NPCs and reading notes, letters, books and other documents.
- The phone is the offline menu and holds notes and discoveries.
- **No voicemails from his father.**

**Proposed: consequences**
- The ferry crossing, the arrival and the vigil are played, not watched. The camera stays authored, but the player always has control.
- Because the story leans on NPCs, the game needs living people to talk to after the islanders turn. Section 6 suggests freed islanders gathering at the safe areas.
- The phone still gets texts from his mother, the ferry company and the coastguard at safe areas. Say if those should go too.

## 3. The opening

**Settled**
1. **The ferry.** The game starts on the crossing. All we know is that he's going to his father's funeral.
2. **Arrival.** He gets off onto the main island. Everything seems normal, with a few small strange things.
3. **The father's house** on the tidal islet with a lighthouse. This is the main hub.
4. **The vigil.** He has to sit by his father's coffin, but he doesn't know the rituals. Nothing appears to be wrong.
5. **The kirk service.** His father's body is gone or taken, and the islanders turn.
6. **The weapon sequence:** the phone torch only (retreat), then a kitchen knife that proves useless, then a book, note or log revealing that only the ancient sword in the kirk can stop them. Taking the sword ends the opening.
7. **The tide is the threat.** If he stays on the main island too long, he can't get back to the Brough until the tide turns, so he has to shelter and wait it out.

**Working plan**
- The kirk sword hangs above his father's coffin at the service. The answer is in plain sight in the first scene of horror.
- On arrival an old neighbour mentions that the funeral is timed by the tide ("Service is at ten, low water's at nine"). That's the first tide lesson, in plain dialogue.

**Settled: the vigil as play.** *(William: "go with recommended".)* The vigil uses real Scottish deathbed customs, each sitting in the room as an object the player can use or ignore, with nothing to say what it's for:
- a plate of salt (traditionally laid on the chest)
- the mirror (traditionally covered)
- the clock (traditionally stopped)
- the window (traditionally opened to let the soul out)
- the fire and the candle (kept burning all night)

There's no tutorial and no fail screen. Whatever the player does, he falls asleep before dawn and the candle gutters out. Later, his father's notebook explains each rite, and the player realises exactly what they missed. That way the guilt is the player's own, not just the character's. It could also feed the endings: you learn the watch properly by the end, having failed it at the start.

**Proposed: small strange things on arrival**
- Everyone knows who he is before he says it.
- Salt lines on doorsteps, and rowan and red thread over the shop door.
- Islanders stopping mid-sentence to listen to the ground.
- A fishing boat in the harbour with nobody aboard and the engine still warm.

## 4. The protagonist

**Settled**
- **William Sloan**, the son. His father is **Alan Sloan**. *(v3.4)*
- Lived on the island until five, when his mother took him to a large, unnamed city and never let him go back.
- He loved his father and never fell out with him.
- He wrote letters to his father and never got a reply. By sixteen his father was as good as dead to him, and he never visited.
- He never hated his mother for taking him away.
- He takes one pill a day as a routine. In play it's a free choice with no consequences for now (section 9b).

**Proposed:** mid-thirties.

**Open:** his face, and his life in the city.

## 5. The father and the mother

**Settled**
- The father was loving but somewhat strict, an alcoholic, and had served in the British Army. His parents died when he was fifteen. The player learns this from notes and islanders.
- The mother took the boy at five, never let him return, and let him write letters. No reply ever came.

**Working plan**
- **The letters.** The son finds every letter he wrote, worn from rereading, and a bundle of replies his father wrote and never sent. They turn up a few at a time across the game.
- **Orphaned at fifteen, a soldier at sixteen.** The son gave up on him at sixteen too.
- **The grandparents** were lost at sea and may be among the Drowned.
- **The watch.** After the army the father came home and kept the island's dead at rest for thirty years: the salt, the names, the vigils.
- **The drink and the pill.** The father carried it alone with a bottle. The son has his pill and a mother who checks in. The game shows this and never preaches it.
- **Why the mother left.** One night when the boy was five, his father was drunk on the watch and the boy wandered onto the causeway as the tide came in. The memory comes back in pieces, and the full version is a late beat.

**Settled: how the father died is a mystery until late.** *(v3.4)* Islanders each tell a different version (the drink, the sea, his heart in the chair by the window), and the truth turns up late, in his own hand. The actual cause is still to be decided.

## 6. The islanders

**Settled.** They turn hostile at the kirk service. Some of the father's story comes from islanders.

**Working plan: taken, not dead.** The island's dead have taken hold of the living. The sword drives them out, and the Rite speaks the dead one's name and lays it to rest. The islander collapses, alive.

**Proposed: the freed become the cast.** Freed islanders make their way to the nearest safe area (the cottage at first, then the community hall). Each one becomes an NPC with their own piece of the father's story, the island's history, or a hint. This is how the story keeps being told by people after everyone has turned, without cutscenes. Possible faces: the old neighbour, the minister, the publican who served the father, an army friend, the GP, and the ferry's harbour master. Some could be bosses first and NPCs after.

## 7. Map and route

**Settled**
- **After the first boss fight, the ferry pier can't be used.** It doesn't have to be physically destroyed, only incredibly dangerous to go near.
- **He gets an email from the ferry company** saying the ferry has been rerouted to another town across the island.
- The player then has to reach the far side of the island, the only other place the ferry can come in. It works like Orkney, where the ferries only use Stromness or Kirkwall.
- The final journey to that harbour is earned by opening the map through bosses and puzzles.

**Proposed: the other harbour.** A second, bigger harbour town on the far side, past the Hall, with the ferry terminal, a harbour office and a lifeboat station. You see it across the bay from early on. It's the last area you reach, and the ending happens there.

**Proposed: places.** The Brough (the father's cottage, the lighthouse, Norse chapel ruins), the village and the old pier, the kirk and heritage centre, the Howe (a Neolithic cairn under the kirk's mound), the shore with kelp kilns and a fish farm, the loch, mill and wind turbine, the mast hill and fairy knowe with standing stones, a wartime battery and bunkers, the Hall (the laird's house, now a failed hotel), the pub where the father drank, and the grandparents' grave in the kirkyard.

**Settled: why the pier is unusable.** William's idea that the dead boss's corpse releases a curse, built out as below. *(William: "go with recommended".)*
- **Boss 1 is the Hogboon, fought at the pier.** After you take the sword it rises from the Howe to get it back and hunts you down to the waterfront. You lay it low on the pier, but you can't give it the Rite, because nobody knows its name. A mound-dweller older than names can't be laid to rest.
- **Its corpse curses the pier.** The barrow-earth body sinks into the pier and bleeds a black haar over the harbour. Inside the haar your Resolve drains fast and the Drowned gather. You can see the pier from the village for the rest of the game, but you can't use it.
- **Its name as a late thread (optional).** If the runes in the Howe give up the Hogboon's name, you could go back and lay it to rest. That would lift the curse and open the old pier as a shortcut, though the ferry still goes to the other town.

## 8. Lore (all proposed, carried over)

- **The sword:** a Norse blade from a warrior's grave under the kirk floor, found in the 1800s. *"Iron from the howe. Nothing else will lay them."*
- **The dead:** they rest only when a Rite speaks their name. Names (the war memorial, the kirkyard, the notebook) are the spine of the mystery.
- **Why now:** the father's death ended his watch, and his son's failed vigil left the door open. (This fits your note that things go wrong at the vigil.) The winter storm, Teran, is the pressure behind it.
- **The sea:** the firths carry the drowned of every Scottish coast here, and the things that follow them. The Finfolk take people under the sea. The Nuckelavee stalks the shore, can't cross fresh running water, and can't be killed until the finale.
- **Threads named but unwritten:** a woman burned as a witch in the 1600s whose name keeps coming up, the selkies, a voice on VHF channel 16 at night, and texts from numbers that shouldn't exist anymore.
- **The army link:** the wartime battery holds the father's service story.

## 9. Themes

**Settled.** Depression, grief, growing up, and what it means to be an adult, with "one pill a day".

**Proposed**
- **Grief:** laying the dead to rest by name is mourning made into play.
- **Depression:** Resolve is the capacity to keep going. When it's low the world goes flat and heavy.
- **Growing up:** finding out your parents were people with reasons, and choosing what to do with what they left you. The two endings are that choice.
- **Care is a daily practice:** the father's rites, the son's pill, small repeated acts that keep the dark at bay.

### 9b. One pill a day

**Settled (William, v4)**
- **For now it's a free daily choice with no consequences.** Once a day William can take his pill or not, and nothing in play changes either way. William may revisit this later.
- In character, it's part of his routine. Part of him almost fears missing it or running out, in case he slips back into depression and anxiety. That can come through in his own lines and his mother's texts, without any mechanical effect.
- It's never the villain, and never tied to the endings.

**Parked ideas for later.** These were discussed but are not planned:
- Missing one makes the next day slightly heavier.
- Running out or refusing builds a heaviness over days: his nerve sinks, colour drains, and sound muffles.
- Anxiety drowns out the island: his heartbeat and breathing blur the tide's pulse when he listens.
- Discontinuation effects such as dizziness and "zaps" (needs review by people who've lived it).
- His phone notes get shorter and harsher.
- People notice: his mother, and the freed islanders.
- His father's whisky as the dark mirror: a short rush, then a heavier crash (sensitive).
- The GP surgery as a mission for more pills, with a new supply taking days to work.
- A trow stealing the blister pack.

## 10. The endings

**Settled**
- There are two endings: **return home**, or **stay and take over his father's watch.**
- **The player never picks an ending.** Choices made during the game decide it.
- Staying has to be earned: he has to learn the watch properly and lay his father to rest himself. *(William: "go with recommended".)*

**Proposed: the choices that decide it.** Each one is a small act of care, done or not done, which is the game's theme made into the ending. None of it is ever shown as a score or a meter.
1. **Laying his father to rest by name.** This is optional and has to happen before the finale. Without it, he always goes home.
2. **Learning the rites.** Finding the notebook's pages for each rite, and going back to perform the vigil customs he failed on the first night (the salt, the mirror, the clock, the window, the candle) for someone else who has died.
3. **How he treats the dead.** Laying them to rest with the Rite, against letting them fall and rise again. The watch is care for the dead.
4. **Keeping his father's routine.** On returning to the Brough he can do his father's small nightly acts: salting the threshold, lighting the lamp in the window, writing the day's dead in the notebook. Nothing forces it and nothing rewards it on the spot. It's "care is a daily practice" made literal.
5. **The freed islanders.** Helping the people who gather at the safe areas, so the island becomes a place he belongs to rather than one he's escaping.

**The pill never counts toward an ending.** That keeps the rule that it's never a punishment or a power-up.

**Proposed: how it plays out, without a cutscene.** The player always walks to the ferry at the far town. On the "Home" path he boards and the island falls behind. On the "Watch" path, at the gangway he stops and won't step on, and the only way the player can move him is back up the road toward the lighthouse. The character makes the choice that the player's actions built.

**Proposed: the last document.** "Home" ends on a letter he writes to his father, in his own hand, as the island falls behind. "The Watch" ends on his first entry in the notebook, under his father's last one.

## 11. Beat outline

Settled beats are in **bold**. Everything else is working plan or proposed.

**Act 1: Homecoming**
1. **The ferry crossing: he's going to his father's funeral.**
2. **Arrival: normal on the surface, small strange things underneath.** The neighbour, the tide lesson, islanders who knew his father.
3. **Crossing to his father's house on the tidal islet with the lighthouse.** The first letters found.
4. **The vigil by the coffin, without knowing the rituals.** Nothing seems wrong.
5. **The kirk service: the body is gone and the islanders turn.** The sword hangs above the empty coffin.
6. **Phone torch only: retreat to the Brough before the tide.**
7. **The kitchen knife: they get back up.**
8. **The note: only the kirk's sword can lay them.**
9. **Taking the sword.**
10. **Boss 1, after which the ferry pier can't be used.** Its corpse curses the harbour. The vertical slice ends here. Freed islanders begin gathering at the cottage.

**Act 2: The island** (the order is flexible)

11. **An email from the ferry company: the ferry is rerouted to the town across the island.** It arrives at the next safe area with signal.
12. Restoring the island's power, which brings the community hall online as a second safe area and a home for the freed islanders.
13. The GP surgery: a locked building. Its story role is open while the pill has no effect.
14. The wartime battery: the father's army years, and the friend who served with him.
15. The Nuckelavee starts hunting the shore.
16. Boss 2, the Trow fiddler, on the mast hill.
17. Boss 3, the Finwife, in the Hall. The Finfolk are behind what took the islanders. Beyond the Hall, the road to the far harbour opens.

**Act 3: Outbound**

18. The tide turns on him: a spring tide that won't fall, a storm surge, and one night when the Brough isn't safe.
19. The full memory of the night his mother left.
20. Finding his father among the dead and laying him to rest by name (optional, and needed for "The Watch").
21. **The final journey to the ferry terminal on the far side.** The finale against the Nuckelavee as the storm breaks.
22. **The ending: he goes home, or he stays and takes up the watch.**
